# Speaker D — recording script

Read this out loud. Everything below is made up — read it as written, don't put
in your own details.

- **14 answers, 14 separate files.** Name them `01` to `14`.
- Press record, **wait one second**, then start speaking.
- Quiet room, phone's normal recorder, held about a hand away.
- Read it like you're talking to someone, not like a textbook. If a word feels
  wrong in your mouth, change it — but keep Tamil words Tamil and English words
  English. That's the part being measured.
- One take each. Don't re-record, don't edit, don't convert the files.
- Send as **Document** on WhatsApp (not a voice note), or Drive/email.

### 01

There are just three of us at home — amma, appa and me. I'm the only child, so
there's nobody to fight with and nobody to blame things on. Amma works at a
bank, she leaves before I do. Appa is in a textile company here in Coimbatore,
he handles the export side of it. My thatha and paati live in the same city,
about twenty minutes away, and we go there almost every Sunday. On weekdays the
house is empty till evening.

### 02

Yesterday morning it was idli and sambar, three idlis, with some thengai
chutney. Lunch was at the canteen — sadham, rasam, one poriyal and a small
vadai. In the evening I had bajji from the shop near the station. At night amma
made chapati with a kuzhambu, and there was some keerai poriyal left over from
lunch. I don't eat much at night. If I eat heavy I'm asleep by ten and nothing
gets done.

### 03

I wake up at seven, sometimes a few minutes past. I get ready and leave the
house by seven forty. I walk to the railway station, that's about eight
minutes, and take the train — four stops, around twenty minutes. From there
it's a short auto ride, or I walk if I've got time. The train is the reliable
part, the auto is where I lose ten minutes. I'm in class by nine.

### 04

Last week I spent more than I planned. A pair of shoes was the big one, one
thousand eight hundred. Then my train pass, four hundred a month. I ate out
twice with friends, about five hundred in total. Phone recharge was two ninety
nine, and I picked up a couple of secondhand books for two hundred. So roughly
three thousand two hundred. My parents give me a fixed amount and I've
overshot it again.

### 05

WhatsApp is the one I'm on all day — class group, project group, and a family
group that never stops. Instagram after that, mostly reels, and I've stopped
counting the time. YouTube for lecture videos and for music. Google Pay for
everything, I don't remember the last time I used cash. Gmail because every
college circular lands there. And a notes app for assignment deadlines,
otherwise I forget them completely.

### 06

I reach college a little before nine. The first two hours are theory, back to
back in the same room. Then there's a fifteen minute break and everyone goes
down for tea. After that it's lab, which runs two full hours and is the only
part I actually enjoy. Lunch is at half past twelve. The afternoon sessions are
hard, everybody is sleepy. If there's a free hour I sit in the library. College
gets over at four.

### 07

Pongal is the one festival we do properly, even though both my parents work.
The day before is bhogi and we clear out all the old things and clean the veedu.
On Pongal morning amma puts a big kolam in front of the gate. We make sakkarai
pongal and venn pongal in a paanai, outside if the weather allows. When the paal
boils over everybody shouts together. We do the padaippu first, then eat. In
the evening we go to the kovil.

### 08

Germany is the first one, mostly for the engineering side of it — I'd like to
see how their factories and their public transport actually work. Also the
Christmas markets, which is a completely different reason. Canada is the second
one, for the landscape, and because two of my cousins are there and I'd have
somewhere to stay. If I had a third I'd say Japan, but everybody says Japan, so
I'll leave it out.

### 09

The last thing I watched was a film on Netflix, a thriller, about two hours.
The first half was tight, they set it up well and I genuinely didn't know where
it was going. Then the second half explained too much and lost me a bit. The
lead actor was very good, the supporting cast less so. The ending tries to be
clever and mostly gets away with it. Worth watching once, not twice.

### 10

*(Shorter — about 20 seconds.)*

I was born in two thousand and three. Near my place one plate of idli costs
twelve rupees, and you get three idlis. Today I woke up at seven, which is my
normal time.

### 11

The moment I get a kaaichal amma decides it's because I went out in the rain.
She makes manjal paal and tells me to sleep. If it's just jaladhosham I take one
maathirai and go to college anyway. If the fever stays for two days we go to the
clinic near our street. I'm supposed to finish the full course of tablets and I
never do, which amma finds out every single time.

### 12

We buy vegetables on Sunday morning at the market near the bus stand. It's
mostly arai kilo and kaal kilo, we don't buy in bulk for three people. Tomatoes
were twenty rupees for half a kilo. Onions are the expensive one now, close to
fifty a kilo. Then brinjal, drumstick, and oru kattu keerai for ten. It comes to
about three hundred for the week. Amma argues over the price and I stand there
holding the bags.

### 13

I've lived in Coimbatore all my life, so this is my oor even though my family is
originally from a village near Pollachi. Our theru is a quiet one, mostly old
houses with two or three flats coming up. There's a small kovil at the corner
and the bell goes off every morning at six whether you're awake or not. The
weather here is the best part. I'd struggle to live in a hotter city.

### 14
