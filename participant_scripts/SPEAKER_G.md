# Speaker G — recording script

Read this out loud. Everything below is made up — read it as written, don't put
in your own details.

- **14 answers, 14 separate files.** Name them `01` to `14`.
- Press record, **wait one second**, then start speaking.
- Quiet room, phone's normal recorder, held about a hand away.
- Read it like you're talking to someone, not like a textbook. If a word feels
  wrong in your mouth, change it — but keep Tamil words Tamil and English words
  English. That's the part being measured.
- One take each. Don't re-record, don't edit, don't convert the files.

### 01

There are four of us — my parents, my elder sister and me. We're a Tamil family
but we've been in Bangalore since before I was born, so my Tamil is a bit shaky.
My father is in IT, same company for nineteen years. My mother taught for a
while and then stopped. My sister started working last year, she's at a startup
and she's never home before nine. The weekend is the only time all four of us
eat together.

### 02

Yesterday morning was idli and sambar — the batter is from the shop downstairs,
nobody grinds it at home anymore. There was coconut chutney also. Lunch was a
sandwich and a coffee at the place near college. In the evening I had a masala
dosa at a darshini on the way back. At night my mother made rasam and rice, and
plain thayir sadham after that. That's the one meal in our house that's still
properly Tamil.

### 03

I get up at eight, which everybody at home thinks is scandalous. I'm out by
eight forty. I walk to the metro station, six minutes, and the metro takes about
twenty five with one change. From the station it's a ten minute walk, or a five
minute auto if it's raining. Door to door it's fifty minutes and it's the same
every single day, which is actually the good part.

### 04

Last week was expensive. I bought a keyboard for three thousand five hundred,
which I'd been putting off for months. Metro card recharge, five hundred. I ate
out four times, that's easily fifteen hundred here. A haircut was four hundred.
And I paid my share of a birthday gift, another six hundred. So about six and a
half thousand. Bangalore does that — everything is two hundred rupees more than
it should be.

### 05

WhatsApp, obviously. Instagram after that, and I've set a timer on it that I
ignore. Spotify all day, I can't work without something playing. YouTube for
tutorials. GPay and PhonePe both, depending on which one the shop has. Gmail for
college mail, and Slack, because my sister got me an internship where everything
happens on Slack. And Google Maps constantly. I still don't know this city
properly.

### 06

College starts at nine and I'm usually five minutes late. First two hours are
lectures, same room, back to back. There's a break at eleven and everyone goes
for coffee. Then a lab session or a tutorial. Lunch at half twelve. The
afternoons are dead, nobody is paying attention including the staff. I've
started using the free hours for the internship work instead of the library. It
gets over at four thirty.

### 07

Pongal is the one we still do properly. The day before is bhogi, and my mother
makes us clear out all the old things even though we live in a flat. She puts a
kolam at the door, a small one because there's no space. We make sakkarai pongal
and venn pongal — in a cooker, not a paanai, which my grandmother would have had
something to say about. We do the padaippu, then eat. In the evening we go to
the kovil near Ulsoor.

### 08

The US, mainly because half my class is going there for a masters and I'd like
to see it before I decide anything. New York specifically. The UK is the second
one, for the museums and because it's a short flight from everywhere else in
Europe. And Japan, which is the one I actually care about — the trains, the
food, the fact that everything works. I'd go to Japan alone. The other two I'd
want company for.

### 09

I finished an HBO series last month, eight episodes, and it's the best thing
I've watched in a year. The writing is the thing — nobody explains anything to
you, you have to keep up. The lead performance is extraordinary, especially in
episode six, which is almost entirely one conversation. It sags slightly around
episode four. The ending doesn't resolve everything and I think that's
deliberate. I've already made two people watch it.

### 10

*(Shorter — about 20 seconds.)*

I was born in two thousand and three. Near my place one plate of idli costs
twenty five rupees, and you get two idlis. Today I woke up at eight, which is my
usual time.

### 11

When I get a fever the first thing my mother says is that I'm not eating
properly. She makes manjal paal, which I've hated since I was a child and still
drink. If it's just a cold I take a tablet and go anyway. If the kaaichal
doesn't go in two days we book an appointment online and go to a clinic. I never
finish the full course of tablets, and she checks the strip.

### 12

We order most of our vegetables on an app now, which my mother resisted for
about a year. It's half a kilo or one kilo, standard packs. Tomatoes were thirty
for half a kilo last week. Onions are around sixty a kilo here. Then capsicum,
beans, a bunch of coriander. It comes to about eight hundred for the week
including fruit. She still goes to the market on Sunday for the things she
doesn't trust the app with.

### 13

I've lived in Bangalore my whole life so this is my oor, though my parents would
say the real oor is a village near Kumbakonam that I've been to maybe six times.
Our area is mostly flats now, and there's a small kovil at the end of the road
that's older than everything around it. There's a park two streets away. The
weather is the reason nobody who lives here ever leaves.

### 14
